The honest bit

This is an illustration of what he can do, not a customer's story. He works while your computer is on. Xero needs your own Xero account, linked once: he walks you through it. Gmail is one account for now, and Outlook on the web works too.

Before you ask

Straight answers.

The developers' questions: sandbox, what leaves, the record

Do I need to be technical?

Less than you might think, and it is new. A double-click installer, then a setup page in your browser that walks you through a free Google key. A window stays open while he runs. Windows and macOS will warn you once, because the installers are not code-signed yet.

Can he send an email or spend money without me?

Not without asking. Sending, spending, publishing and deleting stop and wait for your yes. A mailing campaign is approved once, as a whole.

Does my computer have to be on?

Yes. He works while your computer is on.

Is he an accountant?

No. Receipts are a bookkeeping aid, not an accountant. He will not invent a rate or file a return.

What's the catch?

It's new. It has run on very few machines, and the fastest way to make it good is people finding what's wrong with it. Open an issue; it will be read.

Not yet tried A real Mac. The Intel Mac and ARM Linux builds on that hardware. A real phone pairing through the relay.

How is he different from OpenClaw?

OpenClaw is a gateway that reaches you across many chat apps, with a sandbox and approvals you switch on. Cunning Claw is one assistant on one computer, with the approval cards on from the first start and, on Linux, a boxed shell that hides your keys. OpenClaw has more channels and more sandbox options. The comparison quotes its own documentation.

Is this the Cunning claw from Diablo II?

No. In the game Diablo II, Cunning claws are items for the Assassin. This Cunning Claw is software: a local AI agent made by Dragon Forge AI in Cardiff, named for the dynion hysbys, the Welsh cunning folk. It has nothing to do with the game.

BEFORE YOU SEND

This wall is public. A person reads every card before it goes up, and approves it as you wrote it or not at all. Please leave out email addresses, phone numbers and anything private — the form turns away the first two.

What we keep: your words, the name you give if you give one, and the date. There is no email field and no account, and we never store your IP address.

To stop spam, Cloudflare Turnstile checks you are a person (it loads only when you start writing), and we keep a scrambled code made from your connection with a secret key: one that changes every day, to limit how often one connection can post, and one for each card you back with “me too”, so each connection counts once.

Want a card taken down? Open an issue with its number and we will take it down.
