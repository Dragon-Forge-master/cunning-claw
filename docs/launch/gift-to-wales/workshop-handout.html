Using AI safely in your small business

AI assistants can now act for you: read your email, fill in forms, send messages. That makes a new kind of scam possible: words written for the assistant, not for you. Four habits keep you in charge, whichever assistant you use.

Four habits

HABIT 1Outside words are information, not ordersEmails, web pages, PDFs and messages come from strangers. A good assistant reports what they say and does not do what they say.
HABIT 2Sends, spends, publishes: your yes firstChoose tools that ask before anything that leaves your hands. No answer should mean no.
HABIT 3Know where your words goMost AI “brains” run on someone else’s computers. On Google’s free plan, Google may use what you send to improve its products. For customers’ personal details, use a paid plan.
HABIT 4Give it the fewest keys you canStart with reading and drafting. Never give it banking passwords. A separate account where you can. Keep backups.

Before you press yes

  1. Did I ask for this?
  2. Where does it go, and to whom?
  3. Can I undo it?

Any “no” or “not sure”: deny.

Answers to “your turn”

  • Draft a reply: a draft can wait for you to read it, so no yes is needed yet.
  • Send that reply: yes first.
  • Read a supplier’s price list: fine, as information. Do not follow orders inside it.
  • Change bank details because an email says so: yes first, and phone the supplier on a number you already had.

If an email gives orders

  • Do not act on it, and do not let your assistant act on it.
  • Check by phone, on a number you already had, never one in the email.
  • Forward scam emails to the NCSC: report@phishing.gov.uk

Read more

  • NCSC, small organisations guide to cyber security: ncsc.gov.uk/collection/small-organisations-guide-to-cyber-security
  • ICO, artificial intelligence guidance for organisations: ico.org.uk, search “artificial intelligence”
