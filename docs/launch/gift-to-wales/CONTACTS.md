# Who to approach, and how

Every channel below was read on the organisation's own public website on
**20 November 2026**, and the URL is the page it was read on. Only
organisational routes are listed: press-office pages, contact forms, and
newsroom or team addresses the organisation publishes for that purpose. Where
a page names individual staff, this file does not copy their addresses; use
the organisational route, or the page itself on the day, if a named contact
is clearly the one the organisation asks you to use.

Nothing here has been sent. Addresses change: open the page again before
each send. "R" below is the day the press release goes out. The v0.5.0
downloads it describes were published on 20 November 2026, so R is no
longer tied to a release: it is the weekday Chris chooses once he has
approved the release, a Welsh speaker has checked "Rhodd o Gymru", and
cunningclaw.com offers 0.5.0 (on 20 November 2026 it still offered 0.3.0).

**Rules for every approach** (from [`../v0.3/MARKETING-PLAN.md`](../v0.3/MARKETING-PLAN.md)):
one message, written by hand, to the organisation's published route; one
polite follow-up at most; stop at the first "no thanks". Never pay for
coverage presented as news. The UK's rules on unsolicited marketing email
(PECR) apply to anything that reads as an offer; a press release to a
newsdesk, or a speaking offer to an events team that invites them, is the
ordinary use of those addresses. Check the ICO's current guidance if unsure.

## Optional extra: a two-week Meta ads test, Wales only

Run this only after the press release is out, the flyer is approved, and the
site offers 0.5.0 and works on a phone. It is a test of whether paid reach in Wales brings anyone to
the site at a sane cost, not a launch.

**Budget.** £5 a day for 14 days: **£70 in all**. Set it as a lifetime budget
of £70 with a fixed end date rather than a daily budget, so the total cannot
run over.

**Audience.** Location: Wales (people living there). Age 18 and over. No
interest targeting at first: the question is whether the message works, not
the audience trick. Placements: Facebook and Instagram feeds.

**Creative.** One image, made from `flyer.html` or `flyer-trades.html` at
1080×1080 (not made in this kit). Copy, with the cost in the ad itself:

> Rhodd o Gymru · A gift from Wales. A free AI assistant made in Cardiff that
> runs on your own computer and asks before it sends, spends or publishes.
> Free to download and start, with a free Google key; the AI service can cost
> money beyond Google's free allowance. New, and in alpha.
>
> Headline: An AI assistant that asks before it acts · Link: cunningclaw.com

The Welsh line is to be checked by a Welsh speaker before the ad runs, like
every other use of it.

**What to measure.** Meta's own report: reach, link clicks, cost per link
click, and the comments. Alongside: GitHub's download counts for the 0.5.0
files at the start and end (a rise cannot be pinned on the ads, so do not
claim it was). **Do not add the Meta pixel or any tracker to cunningclaw.com**:
"no tracking" is a promise the product makes.

**Stop rule.**

- **Stop at once** if: an ad is rejected; a comment shows someone was misled
  about the cost or what it does; anyone reports a copycat or scam using the
  name; or a Welsh speaker says the Welsh line is wrong.
- **Check on day 4** (about £20 spent). Stop if a link click costs more than
  £1, or fewer than 1 in 200 people shown the ad click it. These are our own
  thresholds, chosen because past them £70 buys fewer than 70 visits; they
  are not industry benchmarks.
- **End on day 14** regardless. Do not extend or raise the budget without a
  written decision: what was learned, and what the next £70 would test.
