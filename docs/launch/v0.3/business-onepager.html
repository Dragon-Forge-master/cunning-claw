Cunning Claw for your business

An AI assistant that works on your own computer: reading, drafting, sorting and reminding. Before anything that sends, spends or publishes, he stops and asks you.

What he can do for you

Your inboxSign in to Gmail once in his browser. He reads and searches your mail and drafts replies. Nothing is sent until you say yes.
Your paperworkHe reads PDFs, Word, Excel, PowerPoint and scanned letters: invoices, price lists, forms.
Your documentsLetters, quotes and plans land on the Desk, as ordinary files on your own computer that you can open and edit.
Your standing jobs“Every Friday at five, check for unpaid invoices.” He runs jobs on a schedule, with day names in English or Welsh.
The tools you already use94 connectors, including Xero, Canva, Stripe, Slack and Notion. Press “Guide me” and he walks you through connecting one, step by step.
Your phoneA message on Telegram, Discord or ntfy when he needs you, so you are not tied to the desk. WhatsApp Web works through his browser too, and every send waits for you.

Start free, in four steps

  1. Download Cunning Claw for Windows, Mac or Linux from cunningclaw.com. Unzip it and double-click. Everything it needs is inside.
  2. Your web browser opens on a setup page.
  3. The page walks you through getting a free Google Gemini key, in four steps. No card.
  4. Tell him how you would like to be addressed, and ask for something.

Google’s free plan has daily limits, and on it Google may use what you send to improve its products. If you handle customers’ personal details, use a paid key instead: any key from Google, OpenAI, Anthropic or OpenRouter works.

Safety, in plain words

  • He runs on your computer, not ours. No account with us, and no tracking. Your words go to the AI service whose key you use.
  • Anything that sends, spends or publishes waits for you to press yes. No answer in three minutes means no.
  • Emails and web pages are treated as strangers’ words. If one tries to give him orders, he flags it.
  • There are twelve dangerous commands he refuses, whoever asks. Even you.
  • On Linux his commands run in a box that hides your passwords and keys. On Mac the box is built but still being tested; on Windows there is none yet, and he says so.
